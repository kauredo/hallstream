## Facts found

- **Daktronics All Sport 5000**: 19200 baud, 8N1. The frames are `0x16` (SYN), 8 ASCII digits, `0x01` (SOH), a header `"004210"` plus a 4-digit zero-based offset, `0x02` (STX), ASCII data, `0x04` (EOT), 2 uppercase hex digits of checksum, `0x17` (ETB). The checksum is the sum of every byte after SYN up to and including EOT, low 8 bits. Each frame writes its data into one buffer at the offset, and the console sends only what changed; it never sends the whole buffer on connect (pressing STOP on the console does). Field positions (the manual's one-based "Item", so the buffer index is Item - 1) are the same for every sport up to Item 200: clock Item 1 (5 chars, `mm:ss` or `ss.t`), home score 108 (4, right-justified), guest score 112 (4), period/set 142 (2). Basketball adds shot clock 201 (8 chars, `mm:ss`), home team fouls 236 (2), guest 238 (2). Volleyball adds home serve 201 (`<` or space), guest serve 208 (`>` or space), home sets won 215 (2), guest 217 (2), and the current game's points again at 240 and 260. Frames whose header does not start `004210` carry no data and are skipped.
- **Bodet Scorepad**: 9600 baud, 8N1. The Scorepad sends it out of its RJ45 port; the research reads that a PC takes it either as a TCP server (port 4001, revision J) or through a Moxa NPort RJ45-to-RS232 interface in TCP server mode. This plan reads only a serial port; a direct TCP read is a follow-up. Frames are `0x01` (SOH), address byte, `0x02` (STX), CTRL byte, message, `0x03` (ETX), LRC. The LRC is the XOR of the bytes after SOH up to and including ETX, `& 0x7F`, plus 32 if under 32; the manual's shot-clock samples only match without ETX, so accept either. The message starts with two ASCII digits, the message number. Basketball (sport `'5'`): 18 is the clock (`mmss`, or `ssDt` in the last minute) and period (`'E'` for extra time); 30 is the scores (3 chars each, space-padded on either side, so trim); 31 is the team fouls; 50 is the shot clock, which carries no sport byte. Handball (sport `'4'`): 01 is the clock and period; 02 is the scores (3 digits each). Byte positions are in the research doc, section 3, "Basketball" and "Handball". Volleyball and futsal are not verified.
- **Fair-Play**: no published format. One GPL-3.0 project guesses a hockey layout.
- **The serial library**: `go.bug.st/serial` v1.8.0 (the newest tag on the proxy), BSD-3-Clause. Its root package (`serial.Open(name string, mode *serial.Mode) (serial.Port, error)`, `serial.GetPortsList() ([]string, error)`, `Port.SetReadTimeout(time.Duration) error`, `*serial.PortError` with `Code()` values `PortBusy`, `PortNotFound`, `PermissionDenied`) is pure Go on Windows, macOS and Linux. Its `enumerator` package (USB names and IDs) is not needed: a list of port names is enough.
- **How the box works today** (`internal/source/console.go`): `Console` takes frames from `ble.Reader`, writes each to `dados/raw.log` through `Raw` (main passes `w.frame`, `cmd/hallstream/main.go:214`), decodes it with `ble.Decode` into one map kept across frames, and sends the map with `out`. `raw.log` is the score history the game-log upload sends (CONTRACT "Game logs"). `source.Switch` (`internal/source/source.go`) runs one `Source` at a time by `settings.input`; `Use` returns early when the source is already running (`source.go:51-53`). `store.Inputs` is `{"console", "manual"}` (`internal/store/store.go:192`), and `PUT /api/settings` replaces an unknown input with the first (`internal/web/server.go:574`). A changed input calls `UseInput` (`server.go:621`).
- **Console values** are the keys `state.Machine.ApplyConsole` reads (CONTRACT "Score input"). `sport` feeds `Live.Sport` (`internal/state/state.go:930`). `home_timeouts`/`away_timeouts` mean timeouts *taken*, and the overlay draws timeout marks only when `input` is `manual` (CONTRACT line 115, `web/overlay/overlay.js:553`). Both consoles send timeouts *left*, so this plan sends no timeouts. The contract says "Soccer is scored from the phone only; a console is not supported" (CONTRACT line 120), so no soccer either.
- **Capture** (`internal/ble/capture.go`, CONTRACT "Console capture"): `ble.Recorder` writes `# hallstream capture 1 app=… box=… sport=… start=…` and then `<ms> <hex>` lines. `Recorder.Offer(frame)` never blocks; `Stop`/`StopNoWait(reason)` end a recording. The Bluetooth reader offers every box frame to its own Recorder (`internal/ble/reader.go:529-531`) and stops it with `"box"` when the box drops (`reader.go:568`), whatever the input. The panel's switch calls `POST /api/ble/capture` (`internal/web/capture.go`), which refuses unless `s.BLE.Linked()` and uses `s.BLE.Capture`. `-replay <file>` reads frames with `ble.ReadCapture` (it checks the header's magic and ignores its fields) and plays them through `Console.Replay`, which waits for `Run` and paces with `ble.Play` (`internal/source/console.go:108-131`).
- **Panel**: the Marcação tab's "De onde vem o resultado" card is `renderCaixaInput` (`web/painel/painel.js:1516`), two radios `cxIn` saved through `PUT /api/settings`. The first-setup step `entrada` has the same two radios (`painel.js:3255`) and a check (`painel.js:3104`). The chips (`painel.js:1209`), the start readiness (`painel.js:1304`), the capture card (`painel.js:1540`, `painel.captura.sem_caixa`) and the tray line (`trayStatus`, `main.go:415`) speak of the box for anything but `manual`.
- **Website**: `/guides/supported-consoles/` (`website/src/views/Consoles.astro`, strings in `website/src/i18n/{en,pt}.json` under `consoles`) already has the console table, with Daktronics AllSport, Fair-Play and Bodet as `wanted`, a sport table whose console column is "Stramatel console", and a `missing` paragraph that still calls capture "on the roadmap". The home page names only Stramatel (`en.json` `description`, the "Score" feature row, the FAQ). The site states only what ships (`website/CLAUDE.md`).

## Layers

### 1. `01-feat/serial-decoders`
- `docs/research/scoreboard-serial-protocols.md`, this plan, the plans row.
- `internal/cable/cable.go`: `type Protocol interface { Name() string; Baud() int; Split(buf []byte) (frames [][]byte, rest []byte); Decode(frame []byte, sport string, vals map[string]string) bool }` (`Decode` reports whether it had a layout for the sport), `Get(name) Protocol`, `Names = []string{"daktronics", "bodet"}`. `Split` drops bytes before a start byte, returns every whole frame whose checksum holds (a bad one is dropped), and keeps a trailing partial frame in `rest`; past 4096 bytes it keeps only the last 4096.
- `internal/cable/daktronics.go`: a 1024-byte buffer filled with spaces, held by the protocol value. `Decode` writes data at the offset (data past 1024 is cut), then reads the fields of decision 5. Scores are trimmed, an empty one reads `"0"`. Volleyball points come from 108/112, the shared fields the board shows (inferred: the manual also lists 240/260 and does not say which one the console fills; a recording settles it).
- `internal/cable/bodet.go`: messages 18, 30, 31, 50 (basketball), 01, 02 (handball).
- Tests (`internal/cable/*_test.go`): the first frame of scoredata's `raw-J5-output.txt` copied byte for byte as hex (the research doc's rendering loses the spaces; check the checksum `49` against the hex before trusting it), a frame with a bad checksum dropped, a partial frame kept across two `Split` calls, offset `0111` landing on the guest score, basketball and volleyball fields, the shot clock shapes; the Bodet sample `01 7F 02 47 35 30 80 32 34 03 39` accepted (LRC without ETX), hand-built messages 18, 30, 31, 01 and 02 with the LRC computed by the rule, the last-minute clock, `E`; a frame for another sport skipped; a sport with no layout returns false; garbage between frames ignored.

## Done when

With `settings.input` `serial`, a port and a protocol saved from the panel, Hallstream opens the port at the protocol's baud rate, decodes Daktronics All Sport 5000 frames (score, clock, period, fouls and shot clock in basketball; sets, points and serve in volleyball) or Bodet Scorepad frames (score, clock, period, fouls and shot clock in basketball; score, clock and period in handball), writes them to `raw.log`, and the overlay shows them; the panel lists the ports and shows whether the console is being read; a serial session recorded from the panel plays back with `-replay` through the same decoder. Bluetooth and the phone work as in v0.1.8. The website lists both consoles and their sports as "codes read, needs a recorded game", in a PR held for the release.

## STOP conditions

- The research doc's byte layouts disagree with what a test built from them decodes in a way that is not a typo: stop and report, do not guess a layout.
- `go.bug.st/serial` breaks the Linux `CGO_ENABLED=0` build or either Windows build (macOS builds with cgo already).
- A step needs the Bluetooth reader changed.
